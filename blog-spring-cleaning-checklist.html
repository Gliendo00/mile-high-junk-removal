The Spring Cleaning Checklist for Denver Homes

August 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Spring cleaning works best with an actual plan instead of a vague ambition to "get organized." This is a room-by-room checklist built around what tends to matter most in a Denver home after a winter of everything piling up indoors — with clear guidance on what to do with the stuff that doesn't make the cut.

Entryway and mudroom

Winter coats, boots, and gear tend to colonize this space by February. Sort by what's actually still worn versus what's been sitting since last spring, and rotate seasonal gear into storage rather than leaving it in the main traffic path.

Closets, room by room

Pull everything out — yes, everything — and only put back what you'd actually buy again today. Clothing that doesn't make the cut and is still in good shape is one of the easiest categories to donate; our furniture donation guide covers the bigger pieces if a closet purge turns into a furniture swap too.

Kitchen and pantry

Check expiration dates in the pantry and fridge first, then work through small appliances and gadgets that seemed useful once but haven't been touched. Duplicate or broken items are the quickest wins here.

Living areas

Furniture arrangements, décor, and electronics accumulate quietly over a year. This is a good time to decide if anything's outlived its usefulness — an old TV stand, a chair that never gets sat in — rather than working around it indefinitely.

Garage and basement

These usually take the longest and generate the most volume, since they're where things get set aside "for later" all year. If the garage is its own project, our garage cleanout guide goes deeper on tackling it zone by zone.

What to do with everything that's leaving

Sort as you go into keep, donate, and junk rather than piling everything together to sort later — later rarely comes. Anything still usable is worth setting aside for donation before it heads out with the rest.

Once the piles are sorted, a single home cleanout pickup can clear everything that's leaving in one visit — priced by how much trailer space it fills, so a small closet purge costs a lot less than a whole-house pass. Text a few photos and we'll size it up before we show up, or book online to reserve a spot.

Keeping it from piling back up

The easiest way to make next spring's cleaning smaller is a quick seasonal pass rather than waiting a full year between purges. Our seasonal cleanout guide breaks down what tends to accumulate in each season, so nothing builds back up to the same point.

Common questions

Spring cleaning FAQ

What's the best order to spring clean a house in?

Top to bottom and back to front usually works best — start upstairs or in bedrooms and closets, move through common areas, and finish in the garage or basement where the bulkiest items tend to live.

Should I declutter before or after deep cleaning a room?

Declutter first. Cleaning around clutter wastes effort you'll redo once the item is gone anyway — clear a room down to what's staying, then clean it properly.

What typically piles up over a Denver winter that spring cleaning clears out?

Holiday decorations that never made it back to storage, winter gear taking up closet space, and general clutter that accumulated while everyone stayed inside more than usual.

How do I know what's actually worth keeping versus decluttering?

A simple rule: if you haven't used it in the last year and it isn't seasonal, sentimental, or genuinely hard to replace, it's a strong candidate to donate or discard.

Can you pick up everything from a spring cleaning in one visit?

Yes — most spring cleanouts are a single pickup, priced by how much trailer space the discarded items fill, whether that's a small closet purge or several rooms' worth.

Do you take items across different rooms, or just one category at a time?

All of it in one trip — furniture, boxes, yard debris, and general clutter from multiple rooms can all go on the same load, so there's no need to separate pickups by room.
