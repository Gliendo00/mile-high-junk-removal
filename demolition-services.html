What "light demolition" means

Small structures, not structural work

Light demolition covers small, freestanding backyard structures and features — nothing load-bearing, nothing that needs an engineer or a building permit for a full teardown. If it's a shed, a deck, a fence line, a hot tub, or a playset that's outlived its usefulness, that's exactly what we handle.

Garages, additions, and full structural demolition are a different category that requires a licensed demolition contractor with permits and engineering sign-off — that's not us. If your project is that scale, we're glad to haul debris once the structural work is done.

The process

How a light demolition job works

1

Send photos and rough dimensions of the structure — that's usually enough for a firm price.

2

If a permit applies in your city or HOA, confirm it's handled before we schedule — we're happy to work around that timeline.

3

Our crew tears the structure down on-site and loads the debris directly — no separate hauling trip.

4

We do a basic sweep-up of the area and haul the debris to the appropriate disposal or recycling destination.

What we don't demolish

Garages, additions, in-ground pools, and any structural or load-bearing work — anything that needs a permit and engineering sign-off for the demolition itself requires a licensed demolition contractor, not a junk removal crew.

Asbestos or suspected asbestos, and any structure where hazardous material is a concern, also fall outside what we handle — let us know ahead of time if that's a possibility so we can point you to the right resource.

Common questions

Light demolition FAQ

What counts as "light demolition"?

Small, non-structural structures and features — sheds, decks, fences, hot tubs and spas, playsets, dog runs, and similar backyard structures. We tear it down and haul the debris away in the same visit, so there's no separate demo crew and hauling crew to coordinate.

Do you demolish garages or full structures?

No. Anything structural, load-bearing, or requiring a permit — garages, additions, full house demolition — needs a licensed demolition contractor with engineering sign-off. If your project falls into that category, we're not the right fit for the teardown itself, though we're glad to haul debris once it's down.

Can you remove a hot tub or spa?

Yes — hot tub and spa removal is one of the most common light demolition jobs we do. We break it down on-site since most won't fit through a gate or doorway intact, then haul away every piece.

Do I need a permit for shed or deck demolition?

For most small, freestanding sheds and grade-level decks, no — but permit rules vary by city and HOA. If you're unsure, check with your local building department before we schedule; we're happy to work around whatever timeline that requires.

How is light demolition priced?

The same trailer-space pricing as every job we do, from a $100 minimum up to a $600 full load, plus labor for the teardown itself. Send photos and rough dimensions and we'll give you a firm price before we schedule.
