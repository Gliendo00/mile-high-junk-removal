Dumpster Rental vs. Junk Removal: Which One Fits Your Denver Project?

August 8, 2026 · Updated August 10, 2026 · 8 min read

When a project generates more debris than a few trash bags can handle, the choice usually comes down to two options: rent a dumpster and fill it yourself, or call a crew to load and haul everything for you. Good news — Mile High offers both directly, so you don't have to juggle two different companies to figure out which fits. Here's how our 15-yard dumpster rental and our full-service junk removal actually differ, and how to tell which one is the better call for your project.

The core difference

Our dumpster rental gets you a container. We drop a 15-yard dumpster in your driveway or approved spot, you fill it at your own pace over up to 5 days, and then we haul it off. Junk removal is the opposite structure — a crew shows up, does the loading themselves, and everything is gone by the time they leave. One gives you time and control over the loading; the other gives you speed and takes the physical labor off your plate.

Cost comparison

Our 15-yard dumpster is a flat $349, which includes delivery, pickup, up to 5 days on site, and up to 2 tons (4,000 lbs) of accepted material — go over that tonnage and it's $90 per additional ton, or keep it longer at $15/day. Full pricing and rules are on our dumpster rental page.

Junk removal is priced by how much trailer space your items take up, from a $100 minimum to $600 for a full load — see the full breakdown on our pricing page, or our full cost guide for real-world examples. For a single cleanout, that number is often comparable to or lower than the dumpster once you factor in that labor is already included.

Time and effort

A dumpster sits on your property for the length of the rental, which means a visible bin in your driveway for up to a week. You're also doing every bit of the lifting yourself, which adds up fast for anything heavy — old furniture, appliances, construction material. Junk removal wraps into a single visit: no bin sitting around, no lifting on your end, and most jobs are done same-day or next-day.

When our dumpster rental makes more sense

If you're mid-renovation and generating debris continuously over several days or weeks — framing scraps, old drywall, flooring — having a dumpster on-site to toss things into as you go is genuinely more convenient than scheduling multiple pickups. Our construction debris removal guide covers how we handle that kind of debris when a single pickup, rather than a standing dumpster, is the better fit.

When junk removal makes more sense

  • A one-time cleanout — a garage, a full house, an estate — where the mess is already there and just needs to go
  • Moving out, or clearing a property before a sale
  • You'd rather not deal with permits, HOA approval, or a bin sitting visible on the property
  • You want usable furniture or appliances donated instead of dumped — something a dumpster can't sort for you, but a crew doing the loading can

That last point matters more than people expect. Everything usable that comes off a junk removal job gets set aside for local donation centers and nonprofits before anything is thrown away — a dumpster, by contrast, treats everything the same regardless of condition.

Permits and HOA rules in the Denver metro

If our dumpster needs to sit on a public street or right-of-way in Denver, Aurora, or most surrounding cities, you'll typically need a permit — placement entirely within your own driveway usually avoids this, but it's always worth a quick call to your city first. HOAs add another layer, since many communities restrict how long a dumpster can be visible on a property or require advance approval. None of that applies with junk removal, since nothing stays behind after the crew leaves.

The bottom line

If you're generating debris steadily over a multi-day project, our dumpster rental is probably the right call. If you've got a cleanout that just needs to disappear — this week, without you doing the lifting — junk removal is almost always the faster, simpler option. Not sure which fits your situation? Call or text us and we'll tell you honestly, even if that means pointing you toward the dumpster instead of a job for our crew. Ready either way? You can book online now.

Common questions

Dumpster vs. junk removal FAQ

Does Mile High Junk Removal rent dumpsters directly?

Yes — we offer a 15-yard dumpster for a flat $349, which includes delivery, pickup, up to 5 days on site, and up to 2 tons of accepted material. See our dumpster rental page for full details.

Do I need a permit to put a dumpster on my property in the Denver metro?

Usually not if it's placed entirely on your own driveway. If it needs to sit on a public street or right-of-way, most cities require a permit — worth a quick call to your city before delivery day.

How long can I keep the rented dumpster?

Our rental includes 5 days, with extra days available at $15/day if your project runs long. That clock runs whether you're actively filling it or not, so it's worth timing delivery to when you're ready to start.

What happens if I overfill or exceed the weight limit on the dumpster?

Our 15-yard dumpster includes 2 tons (4,000 lbs); additional weight is $90 per ton, prorated to the actual scale weight. Overfilling above the top rail can also trigger a rule-violation fee if it requires extra handling.

Is junk removal more expensive than renting your dumpster?

It depends on the job. Junk removal is priced from $100 to $600 by trailer space and includes the labor of loading; dumpster rental is a flat $349 for 5 days and 2 tons, but you do the loading yourself. For a one-time cleanout, junk removal is often comparable or cheaper once labor is factored in — for a multi-day project, the dumpster is usually the better value.

Can you haul away construction debris the way a dumpster would?

Yes — we regularly haul drywall, flooring, and general renovation debris as part of junk removal, priced by trailer space. For larger, multi-week projects generating debris continuously, our dumpster rental tends to make more sense.

Do I have to load the dumpster myself?

Yes, that's the core tradeoff — the dumpster gives you a container to fill on your own schedule, but you (or your crew) do all the lifting. Junk removal includes the labor of loading and hauling instead.

Do HOAs typically allow dumpsters in the driveway or street?

Many HOAs restrict how long a dumpster can sit visible on a property, or require prior approval. Junk removal sidesteps this entirely since nothing stays parked on-site — we're in and out the same visit.
