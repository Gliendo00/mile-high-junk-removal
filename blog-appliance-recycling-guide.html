Recycling

Appliance Recycling Guide for the Denver Metro

August 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Appliances are one of the few categories of household junk that come with actual legal requirements attached, mostly because of what's inside them. Here's what that means in practice, and what it costs to have an old fridge, washer, or AC unit hauled away responsibly.

Why appliances can't just go in the trash

Refrigerators, freezers, and window or standalone AC units all contain Freon, a refrigerant regulated by the EPA. It has to be professionally recovered before the unit can be scrapped — this isn't optional, and most standard trash pickup won't take these appliances for exactly that reason.

The Freon recovery process

Freon-containing units go through certified recovery before the metal is recycled — the refrigerant is safely extracted using specialized equipment, as required by law. That process is why these specific appliances carry a flat $25 disposal fee, separate from standard trailer-space pricing.

What's recyclable in a typical appliance

Once any required Freon recovery is done, appliances are largely metal, and metal is highly recyclable. Refrigerators, washers, dryers, and dishwashers are all recycled for scrap rather than sent straight to a landfill.

Appliance-by-appliance breakdown

  • Refrigerators and freezers — Freon recovery required, $25 disposal fee, then recycled for scrap
  • Window or standalone AC units — same Freon requirement and fee as fridges/freezers
  • Washers and dryers — no special fee, standard trailer-space pricing, recycled for scrap metal
  • Dishwashers — same as washers and dryers, no Freon involved
  • Water heaters — standard pricing, recycled for scrap once emptied of any remaining water

Working appliances get a second look first

Before anything is recycled, we check whether it's still functional. Appliances in working condition are sometimes set aside for local donation centers when a fit exists — recycling is the responsible fallback, not the default. Our appliance disposal service handles this sorting on every job.

Electronics follow a similar path

If you're clearing out appliances alongside old electronics — TVs, computers, small devices — those go through their own responsible e-waste process. Our electronics recycling page covers how that works.

Clearing an entire kitchen's worth of old appliances costs more, based on how much trailer space they take up together. Common questions

Appliance recycling FAQ

Why can't I just put an old refrigerator out with the trash?

Refrigerators and freezers contain Freon, a refrigerant regulated by the EPA that has to be professionally recovered before the unit can be scrapped. Most trash haulers won't take them for exactly this reason.

What is Freon recovery and why does it cost extra?

It's the EPA-required process of safely extracting refrigerant from a fridge, freezer, or AC unit before the metal can be recycled. It requires certified equipment and handling, which is why these items carry a $25 disposal fee rather than being included in standard trailer-space pricing.

Do all appliances have a disposal fee?

No — only Freon-containing units like refrigerators, freezers, and AC units carry the flat $25 fee. Washers, dryers, dishwashers, and most other appliances are priced under standard trailer-space pricing with no separate charge.

Can a working appliance be donated instead of recycled?

Yes — appliances still in working condition are sometimes set aside for local donation centers when a fit exists, rather than going straight to recycling.

Do you actually recycle appliances, or does everything go to a landfill?

Metal appliances are recycled for scrap after any required Freon recovery. Recycling comes before disposal for every load, not as an afterthought.

How much does appliance removal cost in Thornton?

A single appliance typically runs close to our $100 minimum pickup, plus the $25 Freon fee if it's a fridge, freezer, or AC unit. Clearing out a full kitchen's worth of appliances costs more, based on trailer space.
