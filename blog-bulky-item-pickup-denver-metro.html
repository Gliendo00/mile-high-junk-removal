Bulky Item Pickup Around Denver: What Each City Offers

November 18, 2026 · 4 min read

Getting rid of a couch, mattress or old dresser around Denver depends a lot on which city you live in. Some cities run their own large item pickup, some charge per item, and many leave it entirely to your private hauler or HOA. Here's how it works in each of the cities we serve, and when it's simpler to have a crew carry it out for you.

Cities that run their own pickup

Denver. Homes on City of Denver trash service get Large Item Pickup every nine weeks on their regular trash day. Each address can set out up to 5 large items, like furniture, rugs or exercise equipment, plus up to 10 bags, by 6 a.m. Mattresses and box springs have to be bagged and wrapped, and branches bundled. Appliances, electronics, construction materials and hazardous materials are not accepted, and setting items out on any other day counts as illegal dumping. More detail is on our Denver junk removal page.

Thornton. Thornton's Click & Haul picks up large items by appointment. The fee is based on how many pickup-truck beds the load fills, starting at $72 on your utility bill. It takes furniture, appliances, mattresses and some remodeling debris, but not electronics, tires, concrete or hazardous materials. See our Thornton page.

Arvada. Homes on the city's program, run by Republic Services, can schedule an on-demand bulky item pickup that's charged per item and paid up front. Republic also holds bulky item drop-off events twice a year. See our Arvada page.

Broomfield. Broomfield runs a free curbside Spring Cleanup across four Saturdays in April and May. Items have to be sorted and at the curb by 7 a.m., and the program skips fridges, electronics, tires and anything two people can't lift. See our Broomfield page.

Cities where it's up to your hauler or HOA

In these cities there's no citywide large item day. Whether your hauler takes bulky items, how often, and what it charges depends on your plan.

  • Aurora: weekly service through a city-permitted private hauler, arranged by you, your HOA or your building
  • Lakewood: the city says bulky items can be picked up for a fee by your hauler or a junk removal company
  • Westminster: no city large item program; the Sustainability Center takes some items, like mattresses and electronics, for a fee
  • Englewood: weekly service from a licensed private hauler is required
  • Littleton: you pick your own hauler, and haulers will need a city license starting January 2027
  • Centennial: private providers, sometimes one contracted by your HOA
  • Highlands Ranch: HRCA's opt-in trash program doesn't include bulk items, so larger pieces need a special pickup from your provider

What city programs usually won't take

The same items get turned away almost everywhere: TVs and other electronics, tires, paint and chemicals, concrete, dirt and sod. Denver also won't take appliances at Large Item Pickup. Those are the items that end up sitting in a garage for months. We take appliances and electronics in the same load as everything else, with the disposal fees listed on our pricing page.

When the city option is the right call

If you have one or two pieces, they're accepted items, you can get them to the curb yourself, and your pickup date is coming up soon, the city or hauler option is hard to beat. Use it.

When a crew makes more sense

  • You have more items than the program allows, or you're paying per item for a long list
  • Your next pickup date is weeks away and you're moving, selling or turning over a rental
  • Things are in a basement, upstairs or out back, and nobody wants to drag them to the curb
  • Your pile mixes accepted items with ones the city won't take, like a fridge or TV
  • Your HOA or building doesn't allow items at the curb ahead of pickup

We load from wherever the items are, and donatable pieces get pulled aside where a fit exists. See furniture removal for couches, mattresses and dressers, or same-day junk removal if it needs to be gone soon.

What it costs to have it hauled

Pricing is based on how much of our 14' x 7' x 4' trailer your items fill, from a $100 minimum pickup up to $600 for a full load, with up to 1 hour of labor included. A single couch or mattress usually lands near the minimum. Text a few photos to 303-990-1812 and we'll send back an estimate, then confirm your price on-site before loading. Or book online.

City programs and fees change. Check your city's or hauler's site for the latest rules before setting anything out.

Common questions

Bulky item pickup FAQ

How often is Denver Large Item Pickup?

Every nine weeks, on your regular trash day, for homes on City of Denver trash service. Each address can set out up to 5 large items and up to 10 bags by 6 a.m. Check the city's lookup for your date.

Does Aurora have a large item pickup day?

No. Aurora doesn't run its own residential trash pickup, so bulky items depend on your private hauler or HOA contract, and it may cost extra or need to be scheduled ahead.

Will city pickup programs take a TV or refrigerator?

Usually not. Electronics are excluded almost everywhere, and Denver doesn't take appliances at Large Item Pickup. We take both in the same load as everything else, with disposal fees listed on our pricing page.

Can I put my couch out before my pickup day?

In Denver, no. Items set out on any other day count as illegal dumping. Other cities and HOAs have their own rules, so check before anything goes to the curb or alley.

How much does it cost to have a couch or mattress hauled away?

A single couch or mattress usually lands near our $100 minimum pickup. Larger loads are priced by trailer space, up to $600 for a full trailer. We give an estimate from photos and confirm the price on-site before loading.

Does Thornton charge for large item pickup?

Yes. Thornton's Click & Haul is booked by appointment, and the fee is based on how many pickup-truck beds the load fills, starting at $72 on your utility bill.
