Why it takes a crew

Why hot tubs need to be cut down

Even drained, most above-ground hot tubs are far too heavy and too wide to wheel out through a gate or around the side of a house intact. Rather than tearing up fencing or landscaping to get one out whole, we break the cabinet and shell down where the tub sits and carry it out in manageable pieces.

Hot tub removal is part of the light demolition work we do — the same crew that takes down sheds, decks and fences. If the tub is set into a deck or sits under a pergola you’re also ready to lose, we can take that down in the same visit.

What we remove

What a hot tub removal covers

Above-ground hot tubs & spas

Acrylic and plastic-shell tubs with wood or composite cabinets, cut down and hauled away.

Covers, steps & lifters

Waterlogged covers, cover lifters, steps and the rest of the accessories go in the same load.

Surrounding decks & pergolas

Wood decking, privacy screens and pergolas around the tub, removed as light demolition.

Yard cleanup after removal

Leftover patio clutter or yard debris can go in the same trailer — see yard waste removal.

The process

How a hot tub removal works

1

Send photos of the tub, its cover and the path out to the street, plus rough dimensions — that’s usually enough for an estimate.

2

Before the job, the tub needs to be drained and fully disconnected from power. We don’t do electrical work — if the tub is hardwired, have a licensed electrician disconnect it before we arrive.

3

On the day, we confirm the price on-site before work begins, then cut the tub down and carry the sections out to the trailer.

4

We do a basic sweep-up of the area and haul the pieces to the appropriate disposal or recycling destination.

What we don’t remove

In-ground spas and structural work

In-ground or concrete spas, in-ground pools, and anything that needs a permit and engineering sign-off for the demolition itself require a licensed demolition contractor — that’s not us. We’re glad to haul the debris once that kind of structural work is done.

Common questions

Hot tub removal FAQ

Do I need to drain the hot tub first?

Yes. The tub needs to be fully drained before the day of the job — a full tub is far too heavy to cut down or move safely. If it isn’t drained yet, mention that when you send photos so we can plan the schedule around it.

What about the electrical connection?

The tub must be fully disconnected from power before we arrive. We don’t do electrical work: if the tub is hardwired, the disconnection needs to be handled by a licensed electrician ahead of time. Let us know when you book whether it’s still connected.

Can you get a hot tub out of a backyard with only a side gate?

Usually, yes. Most hot tubs won’t fit through a gate or doorway in one piece, which is why we cut them down on-site and carry the sections out. Send a photo of the access path along with the tub.

Can you remove the deck or gazebo around the hot tub too?

Decks, pergolas and similar wood structures around the tub are light demolition work we handle in the same visit. In-ground or concrete spas and concrete pads are a different kind of job — ask before booking.

How much does hot tub removal cost?

It's priced like every job we do: by how much of our trailer the pieces fill, from a $100 minimum up to a $600 full load, with up to 1 hour of labor included and additional teardown time at $125/hour. Send photos for an estimate, and we'll confirm the price on-site before work begins.

Can any of the hot tub be recycled?

Metal parts are separated for recycling when practical, the same as on every job. Most of the shell, cabinet and foam insulation usually can’t be recycled and goes to the landfill.
