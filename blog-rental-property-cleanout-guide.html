Rental Property Cleanout Guide for Denver Landlords

August 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Every day a rental unit sits empty and unrentable costs money, which makes turnover speed one of the few things that directly affects a landlord's bottom line. A unit left full of furniture, trash, or belongings after a move-out is one of the most common things standing between a vacancy and a new lease.

What typically gets left behind

Furniture too bulky or not worth moving, general trash and packing debris, and sometimes a genuine surprise — a mattress, a broken TV stand, boxes of items the tenant just didn't want to deal with. It varies widely, but it's rarely nothing.

A note on abandoned property

Colorado law sets requirements around notice and handling of belongings a tenant leaves behind, and getting this wrong can create real legal exposure, particularly after an eviction. We're not a substitute for legal advice here — if there's any question about your specific situation, it's worth a quick check with an attorney or your local housing authority before anything is disposed of.

Turning a unit around fast

Once you're clear to proceed, speed is usually the priority. We regularly do same-day and next-day pickups for turnover work, since every day the unit sits full is a day it can't be shown or leased. Our home cleanout service is built around exactly this kind of fast, single-visit clearing.

Working across multiple units

If you're managing several properties, we can coordinate directly with a property management office rather than each individual tenant, which simplifies scheduling considerably when turnover happens on a regular basis. Commercial spaces follow a similar process — see our office junk removal page if you're also clearing out commercial units.

Eviction cleanouts

These get handled the same way operationally as any turnover job, but the legal timing around when belongings can be removed after an eviction is worth confirming with counsel first — the rules exist to protect both landlords and tenants, and it's better to have that squared away before we're on-site.

What it costs in Denver

A typical unit left with furniture and general belongings usually falls in the 1/4 to 1/2 load range, $200 to $350, though larger units or heavier accumulation run higher. Text a few photos and we'll send back a real number, often the same day.

Common questions

Rental property cleanout FAQ

What happens to a tenant's belongings left behind after move-out?

Colorado law sets requirements around notice and handling before abandoned property can be discarded, and specifics can matter legally, especially after an eviction. We'd recommend confirming current requirements with an attorney or your local housing authority before disposal if there's any question about the situation.

Do you need special authorization to clear a rental unit?

We work directly with the property owner or manager of record, and don't need anything beyond confirmation that you have the authority to have the unit cleared.

Can you turn a unit around same-day between tenants?

Often, yes — same-day and next-day pickups are common for turnover work, since a fast timeline is usually the whole point.

Do you work with property management companies across multiple units?

Yes, we regularly work with property managers handling turnover across several units, and can coordinate scheduling directly with the management office rather than each individual tenant.

What about cleanouts specifically tied to an eviction?

We handle these the same as any turnover cleanout, though timing and notice requirements around an eviction are worth confirming with legal counsel beforehand, since those rules can affect when and how belongings can be removed.

How much does a rental property cleanout cost in Denver?

A typical single unit left with furniture and general belongings usually falls in the 1/4 to 1/2 load range, $200 to $350, though larger units or heavier accumulation run higher.
